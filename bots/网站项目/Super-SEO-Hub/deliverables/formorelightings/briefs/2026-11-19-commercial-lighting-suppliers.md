### H2: Which red flags appear when vetting commercial lighting suppliers?
- Catalog certificates that are not tied to the exact SKU.
- Unwillingness to name the factory or to allow a documented factory visit / audit trail.
- Missing IES files, vague "CE/UL available" language, or listings that cannot be verified on DLC / ENERGY STAR for the stated model.
- Quotes that omit driver brand/protocol, LED binning, packing, or inspection steps.
- Uncontrolled substitutions after sample approval.
- Keep this section practical and calm; no competitor names, no invented horror-story statistics.

## 6. FAQ (5 Q&As, 30–50 words each, no invented numbers)

**Q1. What should buyers look for in commercial lighting suppliers?**
A: Look beyond catalog breadth. Ask how the supplier will prove photometric performance, safety listings, and production consistency for the exact SKUs you will order. A useful shortlist also shows who manufactures the goods, who documents them, and who is accountable for samples, packing, and after-sales support.

*(Word count: 50.)*

**Q2. Is a trading company an acceptable commercial lighting supplier?**
A: Yes, when the trading company discloses the factory, provides SKU-specific test files, and can show a documented quality process or factory visit path. If you cannot trace production, listings, and inspection steps, you cannot keep specifications consistent across a multi-site rollout.

*(Word count: 48.)*

**Q3. What documents should a commercial lighting supplier provide?**
A: Request documents tied to the exact model: luminaire safety reports such as UL 1598 or IEC 60598-1, IES photometric files, and, where the project or rebate program requires them, confirmation of DLC or ENERGY STAR eligibility. Also ask how incoming, in-process, and outgoing inspection is handled.

*(Word count: 50.)*

**Q4. How do you compare quotes from different commercial lighting suppliers?**
A: Compare the same bill of materials, finishes, drivers, dimming protocol, and documentation pack, not just the unit price. Include sample-approval criteria, packing and labeling, and a written rule for substitutions. A lower quote that omits photometric files or market listings is not an equivalent offer.

*(Word count: 50.)*

**Q5. What belongs in a commercial lighting supplier RFQ?**
A: State the applications, fixture families, target CCT and CRI, glare limits where relevant, controls, destination-market listings, IES files, sample plan, inspection plan, and packing requirements. Ask the supplier to identify the factory and the quality process that will keep SKUs consistent across sites.

*(Word count: 50.)*
