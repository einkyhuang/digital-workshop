## 6. FAQ (5 Q&As, 30–50 words each, no invented numbers)

**Q1: What is the difference between an LED linear light and a batten or tube?**
A: Linear lights typically feature integrated aluminum extrusions, specialized diffusers, and discrete drivers for architectural applications. Battens and tubes are generally simpler, self-contained, utilitarian fixtures. Choose linear lights for premium aesthetics and continuous runs, and battens for basic, cost-effective general illumination.

**Q2: Can LED linear lights be linked together, and how far can they run?**
A: Yes, many linear lights are linkable for continuous runs. However, the maximum run length depends on the driver capacity, wire gauge, and voltage drop limits. Always confirm the exact maximum continuous run length and connector types directly with your supplier before specifying.

**Q3: What CRI and UGR values are required for office linear lighting?**
A: Specific CRI and UGR values depend on the project standards and visual task requirements. Generally, high CRI ensures color accuracy, while low UGR minimizes glare for screen-based work. Always refer to the specific project specification, local building codes, and relevant CIE/IES standards.

**Q4: How do DALI and 0-10V dimming differ for linear lights?**
A: DALI is a digital protocol allowing individual fixture addressing and complex scene control via a two-wire bus. 0-10V is an analog low-voltage system where fixtures are grouped by circuit. Choose DALI for flexible, zone-specific control and 0-10V for simpler group dimming.

**Q5: What test reports should I request from an LED linear light manufacturer?**
A: Request luminaire safety reports (IEC 60598/UL 1598), driver safety (IEC 61347), photobiological safety (IEC 62471), and lumen maintenance data (IES LM-80/TM-21). For North American commercial rebates, also ask for DLC qualification. Always verify these documents match the exact SKUs you are ordering.
