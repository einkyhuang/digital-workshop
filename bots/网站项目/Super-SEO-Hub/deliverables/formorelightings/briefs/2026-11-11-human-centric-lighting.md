## 3. H2 Structure

### H2: What is human centric lighting?

- Define *human centric lighting* as a design approach that serves visual tasks and the non-visual (ipRGC / circadian) responses to light — not a single fixture SKU or a marketing label.
- Contrast with generic “bright white office lighting” that only chases horizontal illuminance and a fixed CCT.
- Note that a credible scheme combines visual quality (illuminance, color, glare) with, where the client requires it, spectral or melanopic data and a time-of-day control plan.
- Place the hero image below the H1.

### H2: Why does human centric lighting matter in commercial interiors?

- Focus on daytime interiors where people occupy the space for long periods: offices, classrooms, and healthcare work or waiting areas. Use *office lighting design* once in this section; do not write a full office design manual.
- Explain why vertical light, glare control, and color quality sit alongside any circadian intent — a scheme that ignores comfort will not be accepted, whatever the melanopic claim.
- Link [commercial LED lighting guide] for wider commercial fixture context, [architectural LED lighting guide] for interior/architectural adjacency, and [UGR in office lighting] when introducing glare as a comfort issue. Point readers to those guides instead of expanding them.

### H2: Which metrics and standards should buyers use?

- Visual metrics, stated as project requirements, not invented numbers: illuminance targets by zone, CCT (link [color temperature guide]), CRI (use *cri 90 led* once; link [what is CRI in lighting] and [CRI explained]), and glare / UGR (use *ugr office lighting* or *led ugr* once; link [UGR in office lighting]).
- Non-visual metrology: introduce melanopic EDI only as a concept. Ask manufacturers to report melanopic or spectral data using CIE S 026 methods. Do not invent thresholds. [cite: CIE]
- Indoor workplace context: tell buyers to consult EN 12464-1 and/or ISO/CIE 8995-1 for the visual lighting of indoor work places — as documents to follow, not as Formore claims. [cite: ISO] [cite: CIE]
- WELL Building Standard: some clients ask for a circadian lighting feature. Treat it as a market requirement to verify against the current WELL documentation; do not invent point thresholds or say Formore is WELL certified. [cite: WELL]
- IES recommended practice and photometric files as documents and data to request. [cite: IES]

### H2: How do you specify human centric lighting for a project?

- Start with application, occupant tasks, and occupied hours (daytime offices vs 24-hour spaces).
- Specify both horizontal and vertical light; state that vertical illumination at the eye matters for non-visual intent, without inventing lux values.
- CCT strategy: use *3000k vs 4000k* once and link [3000K vs 4000K LED]; do not retell that article. Mention tunable-white or scheduled CCT only as an option to specify, not as a Formore product claim.
- Controls and dimming: link [LED dimming guide]; name the protocol as a requirement (for example, the control system the project already uses) rather than assuming a protocol.
- Glare, CRI, and zone-to-zone consistency: keep these in the same specification, not as afterthoughts.
- Data package: IES photometric files, spectral power distribution or melanopic data where required, and a structured sample review. For factory-side process, link [LED lighting manufacturer guide] once — do not rewrite it.
- Beam and distribution: where downlights or accents are used, link [beam angle guide for commercial lighting] once.

## 6. FAQ (5 Q&As, 30–50 words each, no invented numbers)

**Q1: What is human centric lighting?**
A: Human centric lighting is a design approach that serves visual tasks and the non-visual, circadian effects of light. It is specified through application, occupied hours, visual targets, CCT, CRI, glare control, and, where required, melanopic or spectral data — not by a single product label.

**Q2: Which metrics should I ask a supplier to provide?**
A: Start with visual targets, CCT, CRI, and glare or UGR limits as project requirements. For non-visual intent, ask for melanopic or spectral data using CIE S 026 methods, and check whether the client also follows EN 12464-1, ISO/CIE 8995-1, or a WELL circadian lighting feature [cite: CIE] [cite: WELL].

**Q3: Does every office project need a circadian or WELL requirement?**
A: It depends on the client's program. Some projects only need visual comfort and color quality; others also ask for melanopic metrics or a WELL circadian lighting feature. Treat those as project requirements to verify, not as claims about a fixture family [cite: WELL].

**Q4: Which fixture types work in a human centric lighting scheme?**
A: Suspended linear fixtures often carry the ambient scheme in open offices; panels suit regular grids; downlights handle circulation and accents; track is usually an accent layer, not the workhorse. Request photometric files, control compatibility, and, when needed, spectral or melanopic data for the models you shortlist.

**Q5: What should a human centric lighting RFQ include?**
A: Include application and occupied hours, visual targets by zone, CCT and CRI strategy, glare or UGR limits, the control or tunable-white protocol, any spectral or melanopic data request, the standards the client follows, plus sample, photometric-file, and consistency checks.
