### H2: Which specs decide LED downlight performance?
- Cut-out, ceiling thickness, and housing type: recessed models must match the aperture and the ceiling construction; surface models must match canopy size and mounting.
- Beam angle and cutoff: beam width and glare control change how the light lands on tables, walls, and merchandise; use *beam angle lighting* once, naturally, and link [beam angle guide for commercial lighting]. Do not rewrite that article. Do not invent beam-degree claims for any Formore product.
- CCT: keep correlated color temperature consistent across a ceiling and adjacent fixtures; link [color temperature guide] and [3000K vs 4000K LED].
- CRI: matters in hotels, retail, and other spaces where finishes, skin tones, and merchandise are judged; use *cri 90 led* once, naturally, and link [what is CRI in lighting].
- Glare / UGR: in offices and similar workplaces, downlight choice also affects visual comfort; link [UGR in office lighting] where relevant. Do not invent a numeric UGR rating.
- Driver and dimming: 0–10 V, DALI, or other protocols must be stated on the RFQ; link [LED dimming guide].
- Environment: IP needs for wet or damp zones, and insulation-contact or clearance requirements for recessed housings in North America — present these as questions to ask, never as ratings Formore holds.
- No invented lux, wattage, efficacy, lifespan, beam degrees, IP, or UGR figures; where a number is needed, use `[cite source]`. Workplace criteria can be framed against ISO/CIE 8995-1 (see Section 4) without quoting unofficial lux tables.

### H2: Which compliance requirements should buyers ask about?
- Present these as market requirements the buyer should request and verify — never as certifications Formore holds or as guaranteed listings.
- Luminaire safety: [IEC 60598-1](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/66620) as a general luminaire safety reference, and [UL 1598](https://www.shopulstandards.com/ProductDetail.aspx?productId=UL1598) for North America. Buyers should confirm the applicable product parts, any insulation-contact (IC) or non-IC conditions, and accepted reports for the destination market.
- Ingress protection when moisture is relevant: [IEC 60529](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/2452) is the IP Code method. Ask for the stated IP rating and the test evidence that matches the shipped construction; do not infer a rating from a catalog photo.
- Workplace lighting context, when the project is an office or similar indoor workplace: consult [ISO/CIE 8995-1 / CIE lighting of indoor work places](https://cie.co.at/publications/lighting-work-places-part-1-indoor). Link the UGR article for glare method detail; do not rewrite it.
- Energy / rebate listings when the project needs them: check [DLC](https://www.designlights.org/). ## 6. FAQ (5 Q&As, 30–50 words each, no invented numbers)

**Q1: What does an LED downlight manufacturer produce?**
A: An LED downlight manufacturer builds recessed and surface ceiling downlights for commercial interiors such as hotels, retail, and offices. The factory controls the optical system, housing, cut-out or surface fit, driver, dimming interface, finish, and any adjustable aiming — not just the LED module inside the fixture.

**Q2: Which types of LED downlights should B2B buyers compare?**
A: Recessed general downlights light a space from a ceiling cut-out, trimless models sit flush for a cleaner ceiling, gimbals can be aimed, and surface cylinders or cubes mount where a recess is not practical. Wet-location models need a stated IP rating and a matching safety report for the destination market.

**Q3: Why does beam angle matter when specifying LED downlights?**
A: Beam angle shapes how wide the light lands and how much scalloping or glare appears on walls and tables. Buyers should set the beam from the mounting height and the task, then review a photometric file and a lit sample rather than choosing from a catalog photo alone.

**Q4: Which compliance documents should I ask a downlight manufacturer for?**
A: Ask for safety reports against IEC 60598 or UL/ETL listings for your market, the IP rating method when moisture is involved, insulation-contact or clearance guidance for recessed housings, photometric files (LM-79-style) with matching source-maintenance data, and DLC status if a US rebate applies. Verify every file against the exact SKU.

**Q5: How do I vet an LED downlight manufacturer before ordering?**
A: Check category fit across recessed, trimless, adjustable, surface, and any wet-location types you need, request photometric files, run a structured sample program for beam, CCT/CRI, dimming, finish, and fit, confirm batch consistency, review IQC/IPQC/OQC documentation, and confirm OEM or ODM support if you need custom trims, beams, or controls.
