## 6. FAQ (5 Q&As, 30–50 words each, no invented numbers)

**Q1. What is office lighting design?**
A: Office lighting design is a zoning and specification process. It maps tasks and zones, layers ambient, task, and circulation light, and ties each layer to metrics, controls, and fixture schedules a B2B buyer can specify and verify.

**Q2. Which standards should I follow for office lighting?**
A: Consult EN 12464-1 and ISO/CIE 8995-1 for indoor workplace lighting, and IES recommended practice for office facilities. These documents set the targets; confirm the edition and local adoption with your project's authority before finalizing the specification.

**Q3. What UGR or glare limit should I specify?**
A: Glare limits come from your project standard, not from a single supplier rule. Set the UGR target in the RFQ and ask suppliers to submit photometric evidence. For background, see our UGR in office lighting explainer.

**Q4. Which fixtures are typically used in office lighting design?**
A: Suspended linear fixtures for open-plan ambient, panels for grids and cellular rooms, downlights for circulation and reception, and linkable battens for back-of-house. Track and magnetic systems serve accent or collaboration layers, usually not the main workhorse.

**Q5. What should an office lighting RFQ include?**
A: List application and zones, illuminance targets by zone, CCT and CRI, glare/UGR limits, controls and dimming protocol, and a requirement for IES/LM photometric files per SKU. Add sample review and schedule consistency checks before approval.
