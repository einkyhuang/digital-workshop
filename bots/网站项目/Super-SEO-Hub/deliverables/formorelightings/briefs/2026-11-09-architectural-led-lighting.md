## 6. FAQ (5 Q&As, 30–50 words each, no invented numbers)

**Q1: What is architectural LED lighting?**
A: Architectural LED lighting uses fixtures such as wall washers, in-ground uplights, bollards, and linear slot lights to reveal a building's form, materials, and circulation. It is specified for effect and integration, not only for general illumination levels.

**Q2: Which beam angle is best for architectural lighting?**
A: It depends on the effect. Narrow beams suit grazing textured walls and accenting columns, while wider beams suit washing flat facades evenly. Confirm the choice with the fixture's IES photometric file and a mock-up before ordering [cite: IES].

**Q3: What IP rating do outdoor architectural fixtures need?**
A: Exterior wall fixtures are usually specified with a rating suited to wet locations, and in-ground uplights need a higher rating for burial and standing water. Check the IP rating against IEC 60529 and the project's site conditions [cite: IEC].

**Q4: How do I choose an architectural lighting supplier?**
A: Look for category fit across facade, landscape, and interior fixtures, full photometric files, a clear sample program, consistent CCT and finish between batches, documented quality control, and OEM or ODM support when standard fixtures need adapting.

**Q5: Does CRI matter for architectural LED lighting?**
A: Yes. Higher CRI renders stone, wood, and painted finishes more faithfully, which matters when lighting is meant to show materials. Specify CRI together with CCT and keep both consistent across fixture families on the same project [cite: CIE].
