## 6. FAQ (5 Q&As, 30–50 words each, no invented numbers)

**Q1: What is the main difference between 3000K and 4000K LED lighting?**
A: The difference is visual warmth. 3000K is warm white—cozy and inviting. 4000K is a more neutral, cooler white that reads as crisp and professional. Both are common commercial CCTs; the right pick depends on space function and brand ambiance, not on inventing performance numbers.

**Q2: Which color temperature is best for office LED lighting?**
A: Many commercial offices and meeting rooms specify 4000K for task-oriented work because the neutral white supports clarity and alertness [cite: IES]. Confirm glare control (UGR) and CRI alongside CCT, and always validate with samples under real viewing conditions.

**Q3: Should I use 3000K or 4000K for retail and hospitality?**
A: Guest-facing retail and hospitality areas often prefer 3000K warm white to create a welcoming ambiance and flatter merchandise and finishes. Accent layers can stay at the same CCT for consistency, or be zoned deliberately if the design calls for contrast.

**Q4: What does warm white vs cool white mean for LEDs?**
A: In common trade language, warm white often maps near 3000K, while cool or neutral white often maps near 4000K. Labels vary by market, so RFQs should state the Kelvin value (and CRI), not only the marketing phrase “warm” or “cool.”

**Q5: Can you mix 3000K and 4000K in one commercial project?**
A: Yes—mixing is common when zoning different functions (for example, 4000K work areas and 3000K lobbies). Keep transitions intentional, avoid accidental bin mismatch in a single open volume, and approve samples for each zone before production.
