## 6. FAQ (5 Q&As, 30–50 words each, no invented numbers)

**Q1. What is retail lighting design?**
A: Retail lighting design is a merchandising and specification process. It layers ambient, accent, display, and façade light across store zones, ties each layer to visual comfort and color quality metrics, and defines fixture schedules for consistent multi-store rollouts.

**Q2. Which standards should I consult for retail lighting?**
A: Consult IES RP-2 for retail lighting targets, EN 12464-1 for staff and cash wrap areas, and CIE 13.3 or CIE 224 for color rendering metrics. These documents set the baseline; confirm local adoption with your project authority before finalizing.

**Q3. How do beam angles affect retail merchandise displays?**
A: Narrow beam angles concentrate light to highlight small, high-value items like jewelry, while wider beams provide even coverage for apparel racks. Selecting the right beam angle lighting ensures products are illuminated effectively without creating harsh glare for shoppers.

**Q4. Which fixtures are essential in a retail lighting plan?**
A: A complete plan uses track spots and magnetic track for flexible accent, recessed adjustable downlights for clean ambient light, high-CRI LED strips for shelf display layers, and pendants for feature zones. Flexibility is key for future re-merchandising.

**Q5. What should a retail lighting RFQ include?**
A: Include floor plans and zone types, qualitative contrast goals, CCT and CRI requirements, glare limits, controls and dimming protocols, and a strict requirement for IES/LM photometric files per SKU. Add sample review and LED binning consistency checks.
