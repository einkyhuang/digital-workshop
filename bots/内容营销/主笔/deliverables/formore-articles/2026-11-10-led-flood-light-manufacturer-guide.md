An LED flood light manufacturer builds high-output fixtures for sports fields, yards, facades, and industrial sites, where beam control, thermal design, and ingress protection decide whether a project succeeds. Buyers should define the application first, then lock beam angle, CCT, IP/IK rating, surge protection, and driver choice before requesting samples and photometric files.

## What does an LED flood light manufacturer do?

An LED flood light manufacturer designs and builds directional high-output luminaires for area, sports, security, facade, and industrial sites, rather than treating flood lights as a minor catalog add-on. Buyers should expect the factory to control the housing and thermal path, optics and reflectors, driver selection, sealing and gaskets, mounting hardware, and finishes.

That is a narrower scope than general LED manufacturing. For factory evaluation beyond this category, use the [LED lighting manufacturer guide](/insights/led-lighting-manufacturer-guide); this article stays on flood lights. Wider interior applications are covered in the [commercial LED lighting guide](/insights/commercial-led-lighting-guide), and site-level outdoor context in the [outdoor lighting guide](/insights/outdoor-lighting).

The rest of this guide uses four families: modular high-power, slim industrial, compact security, and solar flood lights.

## Which types of LED flood lights are there?

Choose the family by the area you are lighting and the power source, then lock optics and mounting. Modular high-power units suit large fields and yards. Slim industrial units suit building-mounted wash and industrial exteriors. Compact security units suit perimeters and entrances. Solar flood lights suit off-grid sites. Compare those limits in the table before you write an RFQ.

Modular high-power flood lights are the usual starting point for sports fields, large yards, and port or logistics areas. Coverage depends on optic type and aiming, not on housing size alone.

Slim industrial flood lights are often mounted on warehouse walls, yards, and facades. In a slim housing, driver location, heat path, and gasket design deserve as much attention as the optic. Where the goal is architectural facade effect rather than area lighting, use the [architectural LED lighting guide](/insights/architectural-led-lighting-guide) alongside this category.

Compact security flood lights serve perimeters, entrances, and building protection; specify beam and shielding to limit spill beyond the property line.

Solar flood lights serve off-grid yards, remote sites, and temporary areas. Examples include a [smart solar flood light with remote control](/products/smart-solar-flood-light-with-remote-control), a [heavy-duty industrial solar flood light](/products/heavy-duty-industrial-solar-flood-light), and a [compact solar security flood light](/products/compact-solar-security-flood-light). A solar street light is a related outdoor category covered separately in the [solar street light manufacturer guide](/insights/solar-street-light-manufacturer-guide). For indoor industrial volumes, a [UFO high bay LED light 200W/150W](/products/ufo-high-bay-led-light-200w150w) is an adjacent fixture type, not a flood light substitute.

| Flood light type | Typical use | Key specs to define | Common pitfall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Modular high-power | Sports fields, large yards, port and logistics areas | Beam angle and optics, glare control, thermal design, surge protection | Uneven coverage or glare from wrong optic and aiming |
| Slim industrial | Warehouses, facade wash, industrial exteriors | IP/IK rating, driver location, mounting brackets, finish | Driver heat and water ingress in slim housings |
| Compact security | Perimeter, entrances, building protection | Beam angle, CCT, sensor/control compatibility, IK rating | Over-lighting and light spill beyond the property line |
| Solar flood | Off-grid yards, remote sites, temporary areas | Panel and battery sizing, control modes, IP rating, mounting | Under-sized solar system for local sun hours |

## Which specs decide LED flood light performance?

Beam distribution, color quality, enclosure protection, surge handling, thermal design, and the driver work as a system. Specify them together for the mounting height and the surfaces you intend to light. A wattage figure on a cut sheet does not, by itself, establish coverage or durability.

Compare useful output and distribution instead of treating wattage as a proxy, as explained in [lumens vs watts](/insights/lumens-vs-watts).

For beam angle lighting decisions, narrow optics can suit throw and accent, wider optics can suit even area coverage, and asymmetric optics can limit spill. Confirm the distribution with an IES-format photometric file and a sample; see the [beam angle guide for commercial lighting](/insights/beam-angle-guide-for-commercial-lighting).

Keep CCT consistent across a project and match it to the application; the [color temperature guide](/insights/color-temperature-guide) provides the broader context. CRI matters where surfaces, finishes, or people are judged under the light; see [what is CRI in lighting](/insights/what-is-cri-in-lighting).

IP describes ingress protection against dust and water; IK describes resistance to mechanical impact. Request evidence against [IEC 60529](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/2452) and confirm the actual installation conditions. An IP rating alone does not prove suitability for every mounting.

Surge protection should be written into the specification for outdoor flood lights, not assumed. Thermal design (heatsink, housing airflow, and driver placement) affects output stability in service; ask how heat is managed rather than relying on open-ended lifetime claims.

Specify the driver, dimming protocol, and any sensor or remote-control interface together, and check compatibility with the [LED dimming guide](/insights/dimming-guide).

## Which compliance requirements should buyers ask about?

Treat compliance as a document checklist for the exact model and destination market, not as a claim about any one factory. Ask for reports that name the SKU you will purchase; a standard number on a brochure is not proof of certification.

For luminaire safety, request test evidence against [IEC 60598](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/66620) and, for North America, applicable [UL 1598](https://www.shopulstandards.com/ProductDetail.aspx?productId=UL1598) or ETL listings. For ingress protection, request verification against IEC 60529 for the intended outdoor or wet location. CE marking may be required for the EU, and other destination markets can add local rules.

If the project or reseller path depends on U.S. utility incentives, ask whether the model is listed by the [DesignLights Consortium (DLC)](https://www.designlights.org/) and request the listing evidence. For photometry and package reliability, request IES LM-79 reports for the finished luminaire and IES LM-80 data with TM-21 projections for the LED package used in production.

The [DarkSky and IES outdoor-lighting principles](https://darksky.org/resources/guides-and-how-tos/lighting-principles/) call for useful, targeted, and controlled light at appropriate levels, with warmer colors where possible. Specify shielding and aiming, and limit spill onto neighboring property and unnecessary uplight.

## How do you vet an LED flood light manufacturer?

Evaluate an LED flood light manufacturer against the full fixture schedule, not a single attractive sample. Confirm category fit across the flood light types you actually need, then request IES photometric files for every optic, a documented sample program, and evidence of batch-to-batch CCT and finish consistency. Ask how inspections are recorded and how component substitutions are approved.

Use this checklist when you compare factories:

- Review incoming, in-process, and outgoing gates through [incoming quality control](/quality/iqc), [in-process quality control](/quality/ipqc), and [outgoing quality control](/quality/oqc).
- See how a factory-direct process is described under [factory-first manufacturing](/factory-first).
- If you need a custom housing, optic, or finish, confirm whether the partner can act as an oem led lighting manufacturer, and use the [OEM vs ODM lighting guide](/insights/oem-vs-odm-lighting-guide) to settle design and tooling ownership.
- For an led lighting manufacturer china search, pair this category check with [how to source LED lights from China](/insights/how-to-source-led-lights-from-china) and [China lighting sourcing](/sourcing).

A capable LED flood light manufacturer should be able to walk you through these points with files that match the quoted model.

## What should a flood light RFQ include?

An RFQ should translate the site into testable requirements for each area so every bidder quotes the same scope. Separate confirmed requirements from optional alternates, and make any substitution subject to technical review before a sample or production order proceeds.

1. Application, mounting height, and fixture type per area.
2. Beam angle or distribution targets, plus aiming notes.
3. CCT and CRI requirements, kept consistent across the project.
4. IP and IK requirements based on actual exposure.
5. Surge protection requirements for the electrical environment.
6. Driver, dimming protocol, and any sensor or control interface.
7. Finish, brackets, and mounting details.
8. Destination-market compliance documents to be supplied with the model.
9. Sample plan and IES photometric file requests for each optic.
10. Packaging, labeling, inspection plan, change control, and Incoterms.

## FAQ

### What does an LED flood light manufacturer produce?

An LED flood light manufacturer builds directional high-output fixtures for sports fields, yards, facades, and industrial sites. The factory controls the housing and thermal path, optics, driver choice, sealing, and mounting hardware — not just the LED board inside the fixture.

### Which beam angle should I specify for LED flood lights?

Match the beam to the task: narrow optics for throw and accenting, wide optics for even area coverage, and asymmetric optics where spill must be limited. Confirm the choice with the model's IES photometric file and a sample before ordering.

### What IP rating do outdoor LED flood lights need?

It depends on exposure. Exposed area and sports fixtures need a rating suited to wet locations, and fixtures near standing water or washdown need more. Check the IP rating against IEC 60529 and the actual site conditions.

### Which compliance documents should I ask a flood light manufacturer for?

Ask for safety reports against IEC 60598 or UL/ETL listings for your market, IEC 60529 IP verification, CE where applicable, DLC listing status if you sell into US utility programs, and LM-79 plus LM-80/TM-21 data for the model.

### How do I vet an LED flood light manufacturer before ordering?

Check category fit across flood light types, request photometric files and reliability data, run a structured sample program, confirm batch-to-batch CCT and finish consistency, review IQC/IPQC/OQC documentation, and confirm OEM or ODM support if you need customized fixtures.
